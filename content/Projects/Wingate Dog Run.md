# A Dog Run for Wingate

**A dedicated space for dogs. A better-shared park for everyone.**

_Brooklyn, New York | Community Board 9 | NYC Council District 40_

**Status:** Research and feasibility  
**Last updated:** October 21, 2026

> [!abstract] The project  
> I'm exploring the creation of a designated, fenced dog run at or near Wingate Park.
> 
> The goal is to give neighborhood dogs a safe place to exercise while reducing conflicts over shared athletic fields, cleanliness, and recreational space.
> 
> This is an independent community advocacy project. No site has been approved, and the proposal is not an adopted position of Community Board 9.

## Why this matters

Public parks need to work for different people doing different things.

At Wingate Park, residents have raised concerns about dogs using the track and athletic field, including off-leash activity and dog waste. These concerns need to be documented systematically, but they point toward a broader question:

**What happens when a neighborhood has dogs, parks, and recreational demand—but insufficient dedicated space for dog exercise?**

Without an appropriate alternative, different uses of public space can come into conflict.

A designated dog run could help by:

- Providing a permanent, fenced location for off-leash exercise.
    
- Offering an alternative to using athletic fields for dog recreation.
    
- Supporting clearer separation of incompatible park activities.
    
- Providing dedicated waste disposal facilities.
    
- Improving access to a neighborhood amenity.
    

A dog run would not automatically eliminate rule violations, waste, or conflicts elsewhere. Its effectiveness would depend on location, maintenance, design, and community use.

## The geographic access problem

Designated dog runs appear unevenly distributed across Brooklyn, with visible concentrations in northern and western neighborhoods.

Central Brooklyn, including the Wingate area, appears to have fewer nearby facilities.

This observation needs to be tested through an analysis of walking distances, current dog-run locations, and population coverage.

> [!important] What counts as a dog run?  
> This project considers only officially designated, fenced public dog runs.
> 
> It excludes ordinary parks where dogs are permitted, informal exercise areas, and parks offering time-restricted off-leash hours.
> 
> NYC Parks distinguishes designated dog runs from off-leash areas in its official rules and facility directory.

## The Wingate site

Two potential sites have emerged from preliminary map and photographic research.

### Candidate A: Kingston Avenue and Winthrop Street

A large, fenced lawn occupies the southwest corner of the George Wingate Educational Campus.

Street-level imagery suggests that the area is landscaped and does not have an obvious public entrance from the surrounding sidewalk.

**Potential advantages**

- Large, relatively open lawn.
    
- Existing perimeter fencing.
    
- Street frontage.
    
- Possibility of preserving existing athletic facilities.
    
- Potential for a separate public entrance.
    

**Outstanding questions**

- Which agency controls the lawn?
    
- Is it assigned to school programming or recreation?
    
- Can it legally be converted to public dog-run use?
    
- What fencing, entrance, safety, and tree-protection work would be required?
    
- Would the adjacent schools object to noise or changed land use?
    

> [!warning] Site status  
> The campus is associated with both NYC Department of Education and NYC Parks uses.
> 
> Ownership of the broader property does not establish whether this specific lawn is available for a new public facility.
> 
> Agency jurisdiction and school use must be verified before the site can be described as feasible.

### Candidate B: Landscaped area west of the handball courts

A smaller grassy area along Rutland Road, immediately beside the handball courts.

**Potential advantages**

- Near existing recreational facilities.
    
- Could preserve the athletic courts.
    
- Potential access from existing park circulation paths.
    

**Constraints**

- Smaller usable footprint.
    
- Mature trees and root protection.
    
- Proximity to buildings.
    
- Unconfirmed land-use restrictions.

## Evidence that this is not a new issue

### March 2020: Wingate funding request

CB9's Parks, Recreation and Culture Committee agenda dated March 12, 2020 included a request for Wingate Park dog-run funding.

[View original CB9 agenda](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/brooklyncb9/downloads/pdf/2020/agenda-parks-202003.pdf)

This establishes that the project was previously discussed, but not whether it was subsequently approved, rejected, or funded.

### FY2027: Districtwide dog-run feasibility

CB9's FY2027 budget priorities included a dog-run feasibility study, citing increasing pet-related use of park facilities and concerns about conflicts involving children.

NYC Parks recommended funding the study, subject to funding availability.

The same budget cycle included a separate request related to cleaning dog waste and discarded waste bags in parks.

These districtwide requests provide official recognition of the underlying issues. They do not establish the severity of those problems at Wingate specifically.

### A Brooklyn precedent: Kensington Dog Run

Kensington Dog Run, constructed at the Prospect Park Parade Ground, provides a useful model.

Its published project specifications include:

- 7,000 square feet of fenced space.
    
- Separate areas for large and small dogs.
    
- Drainage-focused synthetic turf.
    
- Waste receptacles and water facilities.
    
- A reported capital cost of $452,000 at the time of the project.
    

Kensington demonstrates one approach to integrating a designated dog facility into an existing public recreation complex. Its historical cost should not be treated as an estimate of current Wingate construction costs.

[Prospect Park Alliance — Kensington Dog Run project](https://www.prospectpark.org/paw-some-kensington-dog-run-groundbreaking/)

## Research underway

### Access and population analysis

- Verify the complete Brooklyn designated dog-run inventory.
    
- Confirm the count inside Council District 40 and CB9.
    
- Measure walking distance and time from Wingate to the nearest facility.
    
- Calculate 10-, 15-, and 20-minute walking catchments.
    
- Estimate District 40 residents currently served.
    
- Model residents newly served by a proposed Wingate run.
    
- Create a geographic access map.
    

### Existing park conditions

- Review dog-related 311 complaints near Wingate.
    
- Request available NYC Parks complaint and enforcement records.
    
- Document field use and waste through repeatable observations.
    
- Gather input from dog owners, runners, parents, and other nearby residents.
    
- Identify ongoing cleaning and maintenance challenges.
    

### Land-use feasibility

- Confirm which agency controls the Kingston/Winthrop lawn.
    
- Determine whether the lawn is reserved for educational use.
    
- Obtain available campus site or agency-use plans.
    
- Ask Parks to evaluate feasible dog-run sites.
    
- Determine whether the track reconstruction project offers a coordination opportunity.
    

## Proposal development

The project will advance through several stages.

1. **Document need:** Determine the scale of the existing access gap and competing park uses.
    
2. **Evaluate locations:** Establish ownership, use restrictions, size, and design constraints.
    
3. **Engage residents:** Gather representative feedback, including objections and alternative ideas.
    
4. **Engage agencies:** Discuss feasibility with NYC Parks, DOE, CB9, and District 40.
    
5. **Identify funding:** Evaluate participatory budgeting, Council capital allocations, or other appropriate mechanisms.
    
6. **Track decisions:** Record official responses, requirements, and project milestones.
    

## How success would be measured

Potential measures include:

- Additional residents within a 15-minute walk of a designated dog run.
    
- Number of neighborhood residents expressing demand for the facility.
    
- Continued availability of existing athletic and school facilities.
    
- Maintenance plan and projected operating responsibilities.
    
- Changes in observed off-leash activity at the athletic field.
    
- Changes in reported dog-waste issues.
    

These are proposed evaluation measures, not guaranteed outcomes.
